# Organiser support and participant engagement on event/race platform marketing pages (India focus)

Scope note: research was limited to about 15 public fetches/searches. Several target pages failed (Townscript is shut down; RunSignup and Eventbrite feature URLs returned 404; Insider returned 403; BookMyShow, Hudle, Playo were not reached). Findings below are what was actually retrieved. Fetched-page summaries came via a summarising tool, so exact wording should be re-checked on the live page before quoting.

## Which support channels and promises are standard in India, and what is credible for a new platform?

### Takeaway
In the pages retrieved, the India-facing platform (Wowsly) leads with phone and WhatsApp CTAs and "personal assistance"; global race platforms (RunSignup, Race Roster) lead with a help centre plus "book a call/demo" and publish no response-time promises. Nobody retrieved promised a numeric response time, so a new platform should promise hours and channels it can staff, not SLAs.

### Cited Findings
- Wowsly (India/Australia ticketing, has a sports page) lists phone numbers for India and Australia, a general email, WhatsApp links for demo/team contact, a Contact page and form, a footer Support link, and offices in Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Mumbai and Melbourne. Its comparison table calls its service "Personal Assistance" versus competitors' ticket-based or email-only support. Main CTAs are "Request a Demo" / "Talk to Wowsly Team", both linking to WhatsApp. — [Wowsly sports](https://wowsly.com/event/sports/)
- Wowsly's onboarding copy is "free sign-up, then short setup": add event details, customise page, set up registration, send invitations; a six-step process and a "Watch Our Process" video heading. No "go live in X hours" claim and no stated support hours were found. — [Wowsly sports](https://wowsly.com/event/sports/)
- RunSignup home page: "Help" button in header (to help.runsignup.com), "Support" under the Knowledge Base menu, "Request a Call" in a Get Started section and "Schedule a Call" in the hero. No phone number, live chat, email or support hours on the page; footer has no support links. — [RunSignup](https://runsignup.com/)
- RunSignup positions AI agents trained on its help docs and an AI chatbot on event sites that answers participant questions; it cites customers reporting up to an 80% reduction in email support (a vendor claim). — [RunSignup](https://runsignup.com/)
- Race Roster organiser page: Knowledge base, Contact us, and "Let's talk"/"Book a demo"; support hours, response times and onboarding are not described. A race-director testimonial praises "best in class" support staff. Race Roster pages point directors to a dedicated director@ email described as a priority channel for organisers. — [Race Roster organiser solution](https://raceroster.com/solutions/event-organizer); [search summary](https://help.raceroster.com/en-us/knowledge-base/how-do-i-set-up-an-abandoned-registration-email-campaign)
- Race Entry promises "dedicated customer support from your account manager" for race directors, with no hours, channels or response times listed. — [Race Entry](https://www.raceentry.com/benefits-of-race-entry)
- Eventbrite: Help Center, "Contact Sales" for large/complex events (tailored plans and personalised support), and an Organizer Resource Hub. — [Eventbrite marketing tools](https://www.eventbrite.com/l/event-marketing-tools/)
- Other ticketing vendors advertise a named account manager from day one with onboarding and live-event support (Ticmint), or a dedicated customer success manager for large organisers (Billetto). — [Ticmint](https://ticmint.com/enterprise-ticketing-solution/); [Billetto](https://billetto.co.uk/blog/why-large-event-organizers-choose-billetto)
- Insider's "list your events" page describes attendee/guest-list management, automated reminders to ticket buyers, and settlement to organisers weekly after the event; no dedicated account manager was confirmed. — [Insider list your events](https://insider.in/list-your-events)
- Townscript's site now shows only a notice that Townscript and Townhall services are no longer operational, with help@townscript.com for past queries. Relevant: one well-known Indian event platform is gone, which is a market-opening point (verify date and details elsewhere before using in copy). — [Townscript](https://www.townscript.com/)
- Indian race-day practice: small organisers route queries through WhatsApp (one virtual marathon lists a WhatsApp-only number, "No calls"); WhatsApp is also used for proof-of-completion submissions. — [Townscript event page example](https://www.townscript.com/e/diwali-run-virtual-marathon-330442) (via search summary; page text not independently opened)
- On-ground support in India: an Odisha-based provider markets end-to-end execution (registration, race-day ops, RFID/manual timing, e-certificates) with WhatsApp as the contact channel. — [ironmonk.fit](https://www.ironmonk.fit) (via search summary only)

### Inferences
- Placement pattern worth copying: header contact/help link, hero secondary CTA "Talk to us / Schedule a call", mid-page "Request a call" block, floating WhatsApp button (Wowsly uses WhatsApp as its primary CTA target; floating-button placement itself was not confirmed from the fetched text).
- WhatsApp-first plus phone plus email is the India norm; chat widgets and AI chatbots are a global-platform pattern. A new platform can credibly offer WhatsApp + phone + email + a founder-led "talk to us" within stated working hours (e.g. "Mon-Sat, 9am-7pm IST", only if true).
- Credible onboarding promise for a new platform: a named person walks the organiser through setup and race-day, rather than "live in 24 hours" unless that has actually been done. Offering free setup help (migration of an existing Google Form/Excel list) is a believable early-stage promise; none of the retrieved pages advertised migration help, so this would be a differentiator rather than a norm.
- No response-time numbers were found in any retrieved competitor page, so omitting numeric SLAs is not a disadvantage.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve BookMyShow, Hudle, Playo, Insider support pages, India Running/RaceIndia-type organiser pages, or Townscript's pre-closure marketing. Video tutorials, community forums and on-ground event-support packages from Indian competitors are unverified.
- No documented "go live in 24 hours" example found.

## Which engagement features are most advertised, and which matter most to race directors?

### Takeaway
Across Race Roster, RunSignup, Race Entry and Eventbrite the most advertised organiser-to-participant tools are email campaigns with templates, automated reminders (especially abandoned-registration), promo/discount codes, referral rewards and analytics on channel performance. WhatsApp bulk updates, QR tickets and automated e-certificates are the Indian-platform equivalents (Wowsly).

### Cited Findings
- Race Roster: abandoned-registration campaigns inside its email tool; pre-made template or custom; audience can be narrowed (for example by sub-event); suggests discounts and a "why did you leave" questionnaire; send must be at least one hour after drop-off; example sequence at 1 hour, 1 day, 2 days. The article gives no recovery-rate metrics. — [Race Roster article](https://raceroster.com/articles/race-to-success-with-abandoned-registration-campaigns)
- Race Roster organiser page: targeted/personalised/scheduled emails from registration through race day, built-in CRM reports (retention), special-access and discount promotions, role-specific volunteer communications, partnership/store revenue, and bundled registration (team joins, products, donations). Dashboard mock stats shown: retention rate 54%, 12,596 contacts retained, $230 average revenue per participant (illustrative dashboard figures, not customer outcomes). — [Race Roster](https://raceroster.com/solutions/event-organizer)
- RunSignup: free event websites and free email with unlimited contacts/sends, automated referral rewards, RaceInsights analytics (clicks, registrations, donations), fundraising pages/leaderboards, CheckIn app, GPS tracking, free photo platform, running clubs product. — [RunSignup](https://runsignup.com/)
- RunSignup guidance recommends one abandoned-registration email within 24 hours; Race Entry sends automated reminders after 30 minutes of abandonment (Race Entry's timing is from a search summary). — [RunSignup blog](https://info.runsignup.com/?p=40617); [Race Entry blog](https://www.raceentry.com/blog/articles/get-more-signups-with-effective-abandoned-registration-emails)
- Race Entry: automatic promo codes for volunteers/sponsors, club membership discount codes, loyalty codes for returning participants, early-access codes for categories; Facebook-share-for-discount, referral rebates, invitations on confirmation pages; Review Generation that collects participant reviews and publishes them on the race page. Waitlist and transfers not mentioned on that page. — [Race Entry](https://www.raceentry.com/benefits-of-race-entry)
- Eventbrite: templated email campaigns with open/click/conversion tracking, Facebook/Instagram promotion from within the product, marketplace exposure (claims over 90 million monthly buyers), a dashboard showing which channels drive sales, Canva graphics integration. — [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/l/event-marketing-tools/)
- Wowsly (India): WhatsApp bulk updates for start times/results and ticket delivery ("1L+" updates cited as capability), automated digital certificates carrying timing/category/position, live results leaderboard, photo gallery shared by organisers and participants, polls/feedback, QR check-in, athlete tracking. — [Wowsly sports](https://wowsly.com/event/sports/)
- Participant-side India practice: Chennai Half Marathon states e-timing certificates downloadable within 2 weeks; Tata Mumbai Marathon virtual offers e-medal/e-certificate from the participant web profile; one Delhi half marathon says results/certificates available about 3 days after the event; India Running's Multi City Run lists RFID timing with a timing certificate by SMS. — [Chennai HM rules](https://nebsports.in/chennai-half-marathon/rules); [Tata Mumbai Marathon virtual FAQ](https://tatamumbaimarathon.procam.in/race-categories/virtual-run/faqs); [Insider Delhi half page](https://insider.in/delhi-champion-half-marathon-dec29-2024/event); [Multi City Run](https://multicityrun.indiarunning.com)
- Procam standards state timing certificates from events without verifiable course measurement may be rejected, which makes certificate credibility a trust topic for Indian runners. — [Procam guidelines](https://vedantadelhihalfmarathon.procam.in/race-categories/half-marathon/road-race-technical-standards-&-timing-recognition-guidelines)
- RaceYa (Philippines/SE Asia, not India) advertises free email campaigns, social integrations, live tracking, merchandise/fundraising, shared event calendar. — [RaceYa B2B](https://raceya.fit/b2b)

### Inferences
- Race directors' practical priorities (inferred from what is emphasised and from how each platform sequences features): (1) recover incomplete registrations, (2) one-click communication to all registrants (email plus WhatsApp in India), (3) promo/early-bird/group codes, (4) knowing which channel sold entries, (5) a clean results and certificate output. Ranking is inference, not a surveyed finding.
- Waitlists, transfers/deferrals, volunteer management and sponsor visibility appeared only lightly (Race Roster volunteer comms; Race Entry sponsor codes) in what was retrieved; these could not be confirmed as heavily advertised. Strava link, shareable stat cards and WhatsApp-native campaigns were not found on any retrieved competitor page.
- For India, WhatsApp is the highest-leverage channel for race-week updates and ticket/bib delivery; treat it as the headline engagement tool (subject to WhatsApp Business API template/consent rules, not researched here).

### Gaps
- No data on Strava integration, shareable stat cards, finisher photos or reviews from Indian platforms; no sources on waitlist/transfer/deferral marketing; BookMyShow/Hudle/Playo/Mailchimp-WhatsApp use cases not reached.
- No credible published recovery or open-rate benchmarks found; do not quote any.

## What copy and visual patterns present support and engagement convincingly?

### Takeaway
Retrieved pages use: a header Help button, hero "Schedule a call", mid-page request-a-call block, feature lists with template/composer screenshots, a dashboard mock with retention charts, case-study cards, and (Wowsly) a comparison table and WhatsApp CTAs.

### Cited Findings
- RunSignup case-study cards list named races (Flutie 5K, Harpoon 5-Miler, DONNA Marathon and others) with figures such as sell-out times, repeat participation and fundraising totals; company-level counters (30,000+ events, 10 million annual registrants, $3 billion raised). — [RunSignup](https://runsignup.com/)
- Race Roster pairs named testimonials (name, role, event) with a dashboard-style retention visual and a "partner not just a platform" quote. — [Race Roster](https://raceroster.com/solutions/event-organizer)
- Wowsly leans on large stat counters ("3 Lakh+" tickets, "11 Lakh+" QR codes, "4 Cr+" transactions), a logo grid, and a comparison table; the page gives no time period or method for the numbers and no testimonials in the retrieved text. — [Wowsly sports](https://wowsly.com/event/sports/)
- RaceYa shows organiser testimonials with names of events and "used since 2017" style tenure statements. — [RaceYa B2B](https://raceya.fit/b2b)

### Inferences
- Recommended patterns for GoAthletix (design recommendation, not documented fact): support section as 3-4 cards (WhatsApp, call, email, onboarding walkthrough) with stated hours; a WhatsApp campaign composer mock-up (clearly labelled "sample") showing a race-week message and delivery states; an abandoned-registration timeline (1 hour / 1 day / 2 days, mirroring Race Roster's documented example); a certificate and shareable stat-card preview using sample data labelled as such.
- Wowsly's unsourced counters and self-scored comparison tables are a pattern to avoid for credibility.

### Gaps
- No screenshots were analysed (text-only fetch), so visual layout claims are limited to what the page text revealed.

## What trust elements can be shown without fabricated numbers?

### Takeaway
Show verifiable facts about the product and the team rather than customer counts: named founders, real sample event pages, payment/security facts you can prove, a clear "what we do and don't promise" list, and early-partner language only for real partners.

### Cited Findings
- RunSignup trust items: PCI Level 1 compliance statement, Secure Payment Processing page, fee transparency page, named case studies. — [RunSignup](https://runsignup.com/)
- Wowsly trust items: partner logo grid, data-privacy statements, competitor comparison; none independently verifiable from the page. — [Wowsly sports](https://wowsly.com/event/sports/)
- Race Roster states metrics inside a dashboard visual only as product illustration, and testimonials carry name, role and event. — [Race Roster](https://raceroster.com/solutions/event-organizer)

### Inferences
- Honest elements for a platform with no customers yet (recommendation): payment partner and compliance facts that are true (name the gateway, state what it certifies); published fee table; data-handling and refund-policy summary; named team with running/event backgrounds; real pilot/founding-organiser programme with limited slots (only if real); sample/demo event page clearly labelled "demo"; roadmap or changelog; response-hours promise you can staff; waitlist for early access instead of counters.
- Avoid: invented counts, stock-photo "testimonials", logos of events not served, "Trusted by" strips, "24-hour go-live" unless proven, benchmark percentages from other vendors presented as your results. If a pilot event is run, publish its real outputs (with permission) as the first case study.

### Gaps
- No Indian regulatory or advertising-standards sources (e.g., ASCI rules on testimonials/claims) were reviewed; check before publishing comparative claims.

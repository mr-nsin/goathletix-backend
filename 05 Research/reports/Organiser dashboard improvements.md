# Replace the demo with a real event workspace

GoAthletix should stop adapting the current organiser dashboard and build a real, event-scoped workspace on the data the database already holds. Today's console is a client-side prototype: every number comes from `localStorage` or `sample.ts`, approval is a self-service button, and nothing reaches Supabase or NestJS. Yet the database already models most of what an organiser needs (events, ticket types, waves, form fields, tickets with QR tokens, answers, payouts, broadcasts, staff), with RLS that lets organiser admins read it. What is missing is the thin layer in between: an organiser guard, write APIs, a check-in endpoint, exports, and a front end that is organised around one event at a time. The ranked plan below puts 15 items in the MVP, which together let one organiser run one real paid event from setup to race day: real accounts and permissions, a per-event workspace, a form builder, a server-side registrations table with exports, offline-tolerant QR check-in on a phone, bibs and kit collection, honest home KPIs, and an audit log. Payments are the hard dependency, because orders stay `pending_payment` until ADR-007's provider exists, so every revenue and "confirmed" figure is zero until then and the dashboard must say so. The owner's 2026-11-18 decisions narrow the scope further: each role has its own account (ADR-010), and this dashboard serves organisers only, with no role switcher and no clubs, academies, coaches or sellers content. Several India-specific points (GST, TCS/TDS, DPDP, DLT, WhatsApp pricing) rest on secondary sources and need a CA or lawyer before they are encoded, and many ranking judgements below are the researchers' own proposals, not documented competitor facts.

## The current dashboard is a layout prototype, and the backend is further along than the front end

The organiser area has six routes (Overview, Events, Create event, Registrations, Payouts, Settings) and five nav items, all wrapped in `RoleShell` with no per-event pages at all: no event detail, edit, check-in, comms, team, tickets, coupons or form builder (`FE app/organiser/layout.tsx:5-12`, `FE components/portal/nav.ts:20-26`). The Overview shows four KPI tiles, a 12-week line chart, a "filling up" list, a category donut and five latest registrations. Almost all of it is fabricated by construction: the check-in rate is the literal string "87%" with a hardcoded "-2%" delta, the revenue and registration deltas are hardcoded "+18%" and "+9%", the donut is a literal array summing to 1,620, and the weekly series is 12 constants (`FE components/portal/OrganiserPages.tsx:23-47`, `FE lib/demo/sample.ts:100-104`). Events and Registrations rows are not clickable and have no actions. The create-event wizard is labelled "A stub" in its own copy, validates venue, price and description and then discards them, takes the date as free text, and can only create drafts that can never be published (`FE lib/demo/wizards/forms.ts:5-28`, `FE components/portal/CommonPages.tsx:122`). Registrations show 28 generated rows with no event or category filter, no detail view, no check-in, no export and no answers. Payouts and Settings are components shared with seller and coach pages, scaled by a numeric `SCALE[role]` multiplier. Approval is a "DEMO - Approve" button calling `approveRole`, and the route guard is client-side only, so the demo cannot test permissioning at all (`FE components/portal/PortalShell.tsx:18-25`, `FE components/portal/useRoleGuard.ts:18-33`). The one thing that looks like real data entry, the five-step organiser application, stores PAN and bank number in plain `localStorage` while saying "Nothing leaves your browser" (`FE lib/demo/store.ts:7,44`, `FE lib/demo/wizards/organiser.ts:33`).

The database side tells a different story. Tables exist for organisers and membership (`organizers`, `organizer_members`, `is_organizer_admin()`), KYC and payout account (`organizer_private` with PAN/GSTIN checks and `kyc_status`), events with setup columns and a publish gate trigger (`check_event_publishable`, which blocks a paid platform event without minimum details and a KYC-verified organiser), ticket types (`event_categories`), early-bird tiers (`event_price_tiers`), waves, 14-type form fields, orders, payments, refunds, tickets with `qr_token`, `bib_number` unique per event, `checked_in_at` and `checked_in_by`, answers with an `is_sensitive` flag, coupons, settlements with TCS and TDS columns, broadcasts, event staff and invites, daily stats and results (`BE supabase/migrations/20261001000022_ticketing_private_events.sql`, `...0023_event_setup.sql`, `...20261112000027_registrations.sql`). RLS already lets organiser admins read tickets, participants, answers, payouts, broadcasts, coupons and stats, and event staff read non-sensitive answers only. Two service-role-only SQL functions exist: `create_registration` and `search_participants`, which the data model documents as roughly 1 ms warm at 100,000 participants.

The NestJS side is almost empty by comparison. The whole HTTP API is public event reads, a taxonomy read, one public `POST /registrations`, and `GET /me`; `SupabaseAuthGuard` verifies a bearer token and checks no role, membership or event ownership (`BE src/auth/supabase-auth.guard.ts:22-52`, `BE src/events/events.controller.ts:5-24`). There is no endpoint to create or publish an event, edit form fields, manage ticket types or waves, list or search attendees (the data model says there is "no HTTP route for search yet, on purpose"), check anyone in, export, send a broadcast, compute a payout or issue a refund. RLS has no INSERT or UPDATE policy for organisers on `events`, `event_categories`, `event_waves` or `event_form_fields`; the only events write policies are club-based (`BE supabase/migrations/20260916000011_rls_policies.sql:182-196`). There is no audit table, no waitlist table, no seat-hold release job (`tickets.reserved_until` exists but nothing releases holds), and no organiser read policy on `orders`, `payments` or `refunds`. ADR-007 adds a warning that matters for design: NestJS uses the service role and bypasses RLS, so every organiser query must filter by ownership explicitly (`BE 03 Decisions/ADR-007`, cited in the audit at lines 54-56). Booking itself has only been verified at the SQL level, not through the HTTP endpoint (`BE 02 Architecture/Registrations data model.md:87`), and `Feature status and gaps.md` predates the 2026-11 registrations work, so documentation drift is likely.

## What the best consoles do, and what GoAthletix should take from each

### Registrations: a live, server-side table with a short default view

RunSignup is the most complete public reference. Its participant view defaults to Active registrations, offers about 75 columns behind a "Customize View" picker, saves reports, and gives each row Edit and Manage actions (edit, transfer, refund, remove) ([RunSignup help](https://help.runsignup.com/support/solutions/articles/17000063214)). Race Roster adds bib-state filters (assigned, automatic, manual, modified, unassigned) and saved templates ([Race Roster help](https://help.raceroster.com/en-us/knowledge-base/how-to-navigate-race-rosters-updated-participant-list)). KonfHub, the closest India-built analogue, shows name, ticket type, payment and delivery status, filters by status and payment, and supports bulk approve, cancel, resend and invoice forwarding, with advice to filter before selecting all ([KonfHub docs](https://help.konfhub.com/dashboard/participants)). Luma keeps check-in per ticket and separate from approval status, and shows replaced, refunded or transferred tickets as history ([Luma help](https://help.luma.com/p/managing-your-guest-list)). No fetched page documents duplicate detection or a per-participant audit trail, so those are chances to differentiate, not features to copy. Two competitor weaknesses suggest a design choice: RunSignup filters apply only after an "Update Report" click, and Eventbrite's Check-ins report previews 25 rows ([Eventbrite help](https://support.eventbrite.com/hc/it/articles/34865445475602-Download-your-Check-ins-report)). A live, server-filtered table is therefore an improvement, though that is the researchers' judgement.

For GoAthletix, the default view should be Active registrations, newest first, with Bib, Name, Category, Gender, Age on race day, Mobile, Payment, Check-in and kit status, T-shirt size and registration date, and everything else (blood group, emergency contact, club, ID-proof status, order number, coupon) behind a column picker. The GitLab guidance on pagination makes the technical case against the current in-memory `DataTable`: offset paging at page 50,000 took about 5.5 s against 2 ms for page 1, so use keyset pagination with indexes matching each allowed sort and capped counts such as "10,000+" ([GitLab guidelines](https://docs.gitlab.com/development/database/pagination_guidelines/index.md)). The existing `search_participants` function, which does trigram search over name, email and mobile and exact lookup on ticket number, bib or QR token, is the right engine; it needs an HTTP route behind a guard that checks event ownership. Starter saved views (Unpaid in race week, Bib unassigned, Kit not collected, Missing ID proof, Medical alerts, Under 18 or Masters, Team or club) are proposals, not documented features.

The form builder is the second registrations gap. RunSignup's patterns are the best documented: six question types, per-event assignment, "Ask Each Member" for groups, internal (hidden) questions, drag reorder, and one-parent "Show for these responses" logic where the dependent must come after its parent ([RunSignup questions](https://help.runsignup.com/support/solutions/articles/17000062846-add-registration-questions), [question logic](https://help.runsignup.com/support/solutions/articles/17000062847-setup-question-logic)). Luma deliberately has no conditional logic and drops a deleted question's column from CSV exports ([Luma questions](https://help.luma.com/p/collect-registration-questions), [Luma CSV](https://help.luma.com/p/download-guest-csv)), which is a pitfall for "hide, don't delete". No platform read documents a sensitivity flag or sport templates, but the GoAthletix schema already carries `is_sensitive` on answers, and the product docs specify the builder (preset by sport, locked fields, drag order, live preview, safe renames, CSV excluding sensitive answers) as deferred "until the organiser dashboard starts" (`BE 01 Product/Registration form fields by sport.md:94-116`). Ship it in that shape, with sport presets for running, cycling, triathlon, trail, kids and swim, and with blood group, emergency contact and medical conditions defaulting to sensitive.

Exports are where race week is won or lost. RunSignup offers CSV, Google Sheets, PDF, a Bib Labels builder and scoring-file exports ([RunSignup help](https://help.runsignup.com/support/solutions/articles/17000063214)); KonfHub lets the organiser pick fields, choose XLSX or CSV, and recommends downloading the day before as an offline backup ([KonfHub docs](https://help.konfhub.com/dashboard/participants)); Eventbrite gates its Attendee Summary behind a "Basic reports" permission ([Eventbrite help](https://www.eventbrite.dk/help/da/articles/761718/how-to-edit-and-export-an-attendee-summary-report/)). No public page documents how any platform masks sensitive columns in exports, so protection is open design space. The proposed set is a master list, a printable packet-pickup list sorted by bib with a signature column, a timer start list, a role-restricted emergency sheet, a T-shirt size summary, an unpaid list and a no-show list; sensitive columns excluded by default, every export logged with who, filters and columns, and files delivered as time-limited signed links from private storage. Mobile numbers must be written as text, files need a UTF-8 BOM for Indic names, and cells beginning with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` need escaping against formula injection.

### Race day: a phone-first scanner with an offline roster and an append-only scan log

RunSignup's RaceDay CheckIn app syncs about every 30 seconds, keeps working with poor signal, queues edits and pushes them on reconnect, offers Un-Check In, can lock a device after a set number of unsynced edits, and recommends "Prevent Duplicate Bibs" ([RunSignup app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/raceday-checkin/id1434451638), [RunSignup check-in help](https://help.runsignup.com/support/solutions/articles/17000141615-raceday-checkin-app-checkin-participants-4-)). KonfHub's check-in is a browser PWA with camera or USB scanning, name, email and phone search, offline storage that syncs on reconnect (event data must be loaded while online first), multiple check-in points including goodie collection with timestamps, admin versus user roles, and expiring access codes ([KonfHub check-in help](https://help.konfhub.com/apps/check-in-app)). Neither vendor documents duplicate-scan or offline-conflict behaviour, and no Indian platform's organiser-side race-day tooling is publicly documented, so this part of the design is the researchers' own.

The recommended pattern fits the existing schema. Before the event, the PWA downloads a roster snapshot (ticket id, QR token, status, name, category, bib, size, waiver and medical flags) to IndexedDB. Scans are appended to a local queue with a client-generated `scan_uuid` and sent to an idempotent server function that returns ok, already checked in (with the original time and gate), invalid, wrong event or payment pending. A new append-only `checkin_scans` table keeps every scan including duplicates, separate from `tickets.status`, and the earliest `scanned_at` wins when two offline gates race. Reserved (unpaid) tickets show a distinct "payment pending" screen with a supervisor override and logged reason. The existing unique index on bib per event makes offline duplicate bib assignment fail loudly into a sync-conflicts list. Volunteers get a scoped role (one event, scan and search only, no export), matching KonfHub's admin versus user split, via the existing `event_staff` and `event_invites` tables. Full medical details stay out of the offline roster; only a flag is cached, and the medical lookup is a role-gated, logged online screen. Supabase has no native offline sync, and PowerSync and RxDB exist as heavier options ([RxDB Supabase replication](https://rxdb.info/replication-supabase.html), [PowerSync](https://www.powersync.com/blog/offline-first-apps-made-simple-supabase-powersync)), but a pre-downloaded roster plus an idempotent queue is enough for the MVP.

Bibs, kit and timing round out race week. RunSignup supports rule-based bulk bib assignment, ranges, CSV import and per-person assignment ([RunSignup bibs](https://help.runsignup.com/support/solutions/articles/17000063281)); in India, Tata Mumbai Marathon requires individuals to collect bibs personally at the expo and allows group representatives with documents, while IndiaRunning events accept a signed proxy letter, the QR and the proxy's photo ID ([TMM bib collection](https://tatamumbaimarathon.procam.in/race-categories/marathon/runner-information/bib-collection), [IndiaRunning event rules](https://multicityrun.indiarunning.com)). So the MVP bib tool is ranges per category with reserve blocks, preview-then-commit and logged reassignment, plus a per-ticket kit-collected flag recording self or proxy, proxy name and ID type (last digits only, never a full Aadhaar), with size counts for vendor reconciliation. Timing is a later import and export job: RACE RESULT, ChronoTrack and MyLaps all take CSV participant lists keyed by bib ([RACE RESULT](https://www.raceresult.com/en-in/support/kbexport2?id=51100), [ChronoTrack](https://director.chronotrack.com/hc/en-us/articles/206193590-Importing-Athletes-and-Teams)), and Procam requires net chip time on qualifying certificates and provisional results within 12 hours ([Procam standards](https://tatamumbaimarathon.procam.in/race-categories/marathon/road-race-technical-standards-&-timing-recognition-guidelines)). Column-level formats and DNS/DNF codes for each timer were not found, so GoAthletix must obtain sample files from the vendors the first organisers use. Certificates follow results: RunSignup and Race Roster generate PDFs with name, time, place and logo ([RunSignup certificates](https://help.runsignup.com/support/solutions/articles/17000064293--how-to-create-finisher-s-certificates)).

### Communications, finance and trust: build the ledger and the guardrails before the broadcasts

KonfHub's finance model is the nearest India reference: weekly settlement cycles with gross, platform and gateway fees and net, a GST-invoice toggle with GSTIN capture at checkout, organiser choice of inclusive or added tax, and organiser-controlled refund inclusions with bulk refunds on paid plans ([KonfHub settlements](https://konfhub.com/payments-settlements), [KonfHub GST invoices](https://blog.konfhub.com/automatically-issuing-gst-invoices-in-konfhub), [KonfHub bulk refunds](https://blog.konfhub.com/bulk-ticket-refunds-are-here-refund-multiple-participants-in-just-a-few-clicks)). Eventbrite holds payouts when an event is cancelled or postponed so refunds can be processed ([Eventbrite policy](https://www.eventbrite.com/help/en-us/articles/724340/)). GoAthletix already has `organizer_payouts` with gross, platform fee, refunds, TCS, TDS and net under a CHECK constraint, plus ticket-level `organizer_payout_items`; what is missing is the computation, the provider and the screens. The finance MVP is therefore a per-event sales summary, a settlement ledger, CSV export, KYC and bank status, and visible payout holds, with TCS and TDS lines present in the model but their rules held for a CA.

The India-specific compliance picture is mostly secondary-sourced. Secondary sources report ticket GST at exempt up to Rs 500 and 18% above for recognised sporting events, 40% for premium events such as IPL, and registration thresholds of Rs 20 lakh ([TaxAJ](https://www.taxaj.com/learn/gst-sports-fitness-coaching-services/), [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/ipl-tickets-hit-with-40-gst-watching-cricket-now-costs-like-a-luxury-heres-what-you-pay-now-492413-2025-09-03)); GST TCS under Section 52 at 0.5% ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/cgst-e-commerce-operator-tcs-collection-rate-reduced-0-25-percent.html)); and income-tax TDS on e-commerce operators at 0.1% from October 2024 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/budget-2024-tds-rate-for-ecommerce-operators-cut-to-0-1-from-1)). Whether a marathon entry fee counts as "admission to a sporting event", whether GoAthletix is an agent or principal for s.52 and s.9(5), who is the deductor, and how GST-record retention squares with DPDP deletion are open questions for a CA. For messaging, WhatsApp requires recorded opt-in and approved templates outside the 24-hour window, and billed per message since 1 July 2025, with vendor-quoted India rates that conflict ([WhatsApp policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/hi/policy/), [Meta pricing docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). SMS needs DLT registration of entity, sender ID and templates ([WebEngage DLT guide](https://docs.webengage.com/docs/trai-sms-dlt-regulations-india)). That is why the evidence supports shipping transactional and broadcast email first and holding WhatsApp and SMS until consent capture and provider onboarding exist.

Trust features follow from the DPDP Rules, notified in November 2025 with most substantive duties starting about 18 months later, around May 2027, roughly six months from now ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1707452/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-incremental-tweaks-and-the-way-ahead)). As summarised, Rule 10 requires verifiable parental consent for children's data, Rule 8 requires deleting data once its purpose is served with 48 hours' notice and keeping logs for at least a year, and the Fourth Schedule exemptions are narrow. A sports platform is unlikely to fit them, so plan for guardian consent on under-18 participants, which is mostly a checkout concern, while the dashboard must show a minor's age and consent status and keep medical data permissioned. Supabase's documented RBAC pattern (an Auth Hook writing a role claim into the JWT, read by an `authorize()` function in RLS) suits the team model, with the caveat that claims go stale until refresh, so refunds, exports and bank changes must be re-checked server-side in NestJS ([Supabase RBAC](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/custom-claims-and-role-based-access-control-rbac)). Proportionate roles are owner, admin, finance, check-in and read-only, optionally scoped per event, with an append-only activity log for exports, refunds, bank changes, role changes and views of medical fields. The role set is a proposal; no competitor documents an equivalent.

### Formats beyond races: batches first, brackets later

The multi-sport research found that every tool splits into a generic shell (registration, categories, payments, participants, comms, check-in, reports) and format engines. Toornament models stages, groups, rounds and matches with single or double elimination, round-robin groups, league and Swiss ([Toornament structures](https://help.toornament.com/structures/which-format-for-my-tournament)); Tournify adds divisions, court scheduling and score entry by a unique link ([Tournify format help](https://intercom.help/tournify/en/articles/8909549-format)); TeamSnap generates pools and brackets from a phone ([TeamSnap](https://www.teamsnap.com/tournaments/tournament-schedule-maker)); Mindbody auto-promotes waitlisted clients on cancellation ([Mindbody](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/business/education/product-waitlist-improvements)). Running, cycling, trek and most camp events need only the shared shell plus categories; triathlon and HYROX add legs, stations and waves that can live as ordered arrays in `event_categories.sport_details`; racquet sports and martial arts need divisions and brackets; team leagues need rosters, fixtures and standings. The suggested order is batches and slots (serving camps, swim sessions and workshops), then team registration, then brackets, then a fixture scheduler, then standings. The proposed schema (`stages`, `fixtures` with next-win and next-loss pointers, `competitors`, `sessions`, `enrolments`, `attendance`, standings as a view) is the researchers' design from general knowledge; no vetted reference schema was found. Two time-savers with documented precedent are copy-with-checklist and renew-edition actions: RunSignup lets organisers choose sections to copy and keeps history on renewal, and Luma publishes a "what is not copied" list ([RunSignup copy](https://help.runsignup.com/support/solutions/articles/17000064303-copying-your-race), [Luma cloning](https://help.luma.com/p/cloning-events)). Triathlon leg tooling, swimming heats and federation weight rules were not researched. Because the owner's decision confines this dashboard to organisers, the batch module here means organiser-run camps, swim sessions and workshops sold as event entries, not academy fees, memberships or coach rosters.

### Information architecture and UX: stable nouns, with the event phase driving what Home shows

Stripe's sidebar is object-based (Home, Balances, Transactions, Customers, Product catalogue) with a pinned and recent Shortcuts layer, and its Home surfaces notifications such as unresolved disputes ([Stripe dashboard basics](https://docs.stripe.com/dashboard/basics)). Shopify says an app home should show quick statistics and immediately actionable information, and describes setup checklists with auto-completing steps, a progress indicator, dismissal and "remind me later", capped at five steps ([Shopify onboarding](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design/user-experience/onboarding)). RunSignup separates race-day tooling into its own "RaceDay Tools" section and allows limited dashboard access for a volunteer coordinator ([RunSignup RaceDay CheckIn](https://info.runsignup.com/raceday-checkin-app/)). No reference product documents phase-based navigation, so the proposal here is a synthesis: keep the nav as stable nouns and let a phase chip (Setup, On sale, Race week, Race day, After) change what the event Home shows, which KPIs lead, which attention rules fire, and whether "Open field mode" appears from about 48 hours out. Global nav: Home, Events, Money, Messages, Team, Settings. Inside an event: Overview, Registrations, Tickets and form, Race day, Messages, Results, Money. That reconciles with the earlier role-dashboard report's organiser list and keeps it under eight items.

The home screen should lead with a "what needs attention" list ahead of any chart, because the database already knows the rules: events missing publish requirements (the `check_event_publishable` error lists exactly what is absent), categories near capacity, holds that expired, unpaid registrations in race week, unassigned bibs, unsigned waivers, KYC action required, payouts held. KPIs should be derived, not literal, and the traffic funnel is nearly free because `event_daily_stats` already records views, register clicks and registrations per day. For first-time organisers, NN/g's empty-state guidance (status, learning cue, direct pathway, optionally demo data) and a five-step checklist (race basics, categories and pricing, payout and KYC, waiver and policies, publish) fit well ([NN/g empty states](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)). A "load sample event" option is attractive but must be clearly labelled and bulk-deletable, since the current build's greatest sin is sample numbers presented as real. For power users, a command palette (Cmd/K), saved views, a column picker, density toggle and bulk actions with "select all N matching" are the proposed set, informed by Linear's persistent per-view settings and command-menu prefixes ([Linear display options](https://linear.app/docs/display-options), [Linear search](https://linear.app/docs/search)); these are proposals and no benchmark shows which raises first-timer completion most. Loading rules follow NN/g: nothing under 1 s, a spinner for single cards at 2-10 s, skeletons for full pages, progress bars beyond 10 s ([NN/g skeleton screens](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)), so exports and bulk emails become background jobs.

Accessibility and field mode can be stated as testable criteria. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 sets a 24 by 24 CSS pixel minimum target, SC 2.4.11 requires focus not to be hidden behind sticky UI (fix with `scroll-padding`), and SC 1.1.1 expects chart alternatives that summarise type, trend and data, ideally with a table ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), [W3C 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html), [W3C 1.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-content.html)). Core Web Vitals thresholds are LCP at most 2.5 s, INP at most 200 ms and CLS at most 0.1 at the 75th percentile ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). Field mode's 56 px targets, 7:1 contrast, sub-500 ms cached lookup and the 170 KB script budget are the researchers' internal targets, not published standards. The landing-page research found that IndiaRunning's organiser app defines a `/desktop-only` route and hides its sidebar on small screens ([IndiaRunning organiser bundle](https://organiser.indiarunning.com/static/js/main.85bc350a.js)); GoAthletix should be desktop-first for setup but must not block phones, since check-in happens on phones.

## Ranked roadmap: 15 MVP items, 11 Next, 9 Later

Ranking is by impact on running a real event against build effort on the current stack, using three tests: does it block a paid registration, a safety need or a statutory need; does it unlock other items; and how much of it already exists. MVP means the first release that lets one organiser run one real event. The impact and effort ratings (H, M, L) are the researchers' judgement, not measured. Unless stated, "FE" means a Next.js page or component under an event workspace, and "BE" means a NestJS controller behind the new guards. A recommended default for reads is a NestJS endpoint that queries with the caller's own JWT so RLS applies where policies already exist (tickets, participants, answers, payouts, coupons, broadcasts, stats), with the service role reserved for the two RPCs and for writes, each guarded by an explicit event-ownership check; this answers ADR-007's bypass warning without rewriting policies that already work.

## Fix these existing screens first, in the order of risk

The cheapest wins are corrections to what is already on screen, because several would mislead anyone shown the build. First, remove or visibly label the fabricated numbers. The "87%" check-in rate, the hardcoded +18%, +9% and -2% deltas that all claim "vs last 4 weeks" with no comparison, the literal donut, and the 12-point sales series should either be derived from data or replaced by an empty or sample state with a "Sample data" label on every screen, including Events and Settings, which currently carry none (`FE components/portal/OrganiserPages.tsx:23-47`, `FE components/portal/ui.tsx:44-57`). Delta text should also say "up" or "down", since the arrow icons are `aria-hidden`. Second, repair the Payouts quirks: "Available balance" reads a scheduled row, "In transit" shows a refund adjustment of 38,900 (a deduction) as an inflow, "Next payout" and "Available" use the same row, and "Paid out last 30 days" sums all paid rows without a date filter (`FE components/portal/CommonPages.tsx:87-96`). Until the settlement job exists, show an honest "No payouts yet" state with the KYC status.

Third, make rows clickable. Events and Registrations rows have no detail view, edit, duplicate, publish or view-public-page action (`FE components/portal/DataTable.tsx:72-77`); the first step is a row link to the new event workspace. Fourth, replace the stub create-event: it validates venue, price and description and then discards them, accepts the date as `YYYY-MM-DD` text, supports only one price, and creates unpublishable drafts. Use a date picker, store every captured field, and route to the event editor (M3). Fifth, fix the phone table defect. `td[data-minor]` is `display: none` below 640 px, so on a phone the organiser cannot see Bib, Registered or Period, while the code comment claims the stacked card still shows them (`FE components/portal/portal.css`, `FE components/portal/DataTable.tsx:15`). Show all cells in the card layout and add `role="table"`-preserving markup, since `display: block` on table elements can drop table semantics in some browsers (known behaviour, not tested here).

Sixth, give charts text alternatives. The line chart is an `svg role="img"` with per-point `<title>` children that screen readers will not reach, no y-axis or units, stretching text from `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, and hex colours with no dark-mode variant or non-colour encoding; the donut has no per-segment values in its accessible name (`FE components/portal/charts.tsx:20,30,62-67`). Add a short name, a text summary and a "View as table" toggle. Seventh, stop the shared pages from doubling as organiser pages: `PayoutsPage`, `SettingsKyc` and `CreatePage` serve organiser, seller and coach through a `CONFIG` map and a numeric `SCALE` multiplier (`FE components/portal/CommonPages.tsx:23,119-136`). Organiser payouts need per-event settlement, TDS and TCS lines and statements, and Settings need real organisation profile, bank, team and notification editing, so split them out under the organiser route. Eighth, finish the smaller accessibility items: a static page title on every route, a `<main>` landmark and skip link (the shell content is a `div`), an `h1` on locked pages, a disclosure pattern instead of `role="menu"` links, 44 px touch targets on the phone tab bar (the `min-height: 44px` applies only at 900 px and above), filter and search state in the URL so Back keeps it, an `empty` prop on Registrations, and consistent lock reasons (Events cites KYC, Registrations cites "an active account", though both use the same flag). Ninth, stop persisting PAN and bank numbers in `localStorage` before any real person uses the application wizard. Contrast was not measured, since token values were not read.

## Scope decisions applied: organiser accounts only, no switcher, and where earlier research is corrected

The 2026-11-18 decisions change the dashboard in concrete ways. Each role has its own account (ADR-010), so the organiser console is reached only from `/organisers` and `/organisers/sign-in`, signs in with an organiser account flagged `account_type: organiser`, and never links sideways. Remove `RoleSwitcher`, its "Become ..." entries, the "Athlete view" link (replace it with at most a small "Back to GoAthletix.com" footer link), and every `role === "seller"` or `"coach"` branch in the shared portal components, including the `SCALE` multiplier. The dashboard shows no clubs, academies, coaches or sellers content, and its settings pages do not offer to activate those roles; those roles get their own registration and landing pages. A person who is signed in on a participant account and opens `/organiser` should see a short explanation and a link to the organiser sign-up, not a switch. The ADR itself notes the shell still contradicts it: the switcher "should go" (`BE 03 Decisions/ADR-010:54-57`), and the audit confirms it is still present. Team features stay in scope because they are the organiser's own staff: `organizer_members` for the organisation and `event_staff` for event volunteers. Two edge points need the owner's call. The organiser application wizard offers "club" as an organisation type, which is a descriptor of who is applying, not the club role, and should stay unless the owner prefers otherwise. And the existing RLS policy "Club admins can create/update club events" is a separate path that this dashboard should not extend. ADR-010 also leaves open whether one email may hold more than one account; if so, an organiser registering for their own event as an athlete uses a separate participant account, and the dashboard should simply not surface it.

Reconciling with the two earlier reports requires several corrections. The role-dashboards report recommended one login with a header role switcher and a shared `role_profiles` table; its own banner already marks that superseded, and this report treats the switcher, the "Athlete view" link and the cross-role navigation as removed. Its proposed `organisations`, `memberships` and `payee_entities` tables largely duplicate what exists: `organizers`, `organizer_members` and `organizer_private` (KYC status, PAN, GSTIN, payout provider and account id) already carry that structure, so the MVP should reuse them and add a payee table only if sellers or coaches later need one, which is outside this dashboard. Its statement that paid events publish once KYC is "submitted" is looser than the database: `check_event_publishable` requires a KYC-verified organiser for a paid platform event unless a staff session bypasses it, and the landing-page report's step-4 copy ("paid events need KYC submitted") should be corrected to say verified, or the trigger should be changed deliberately. Its note that the authorisation guard needs organiser membership checks stands, but the audit shows the current guard authenticates only, so that gap is larger than the earlier report implied. Its claim that WhatsApp rules were not researched is now partly closed by the opt-in, template and pricing findings above, though still from policy pages and vendor summaries. Its DPDP commencement date of about 13 or 14 May 2027 is consistent with the new notes, which say the main duties start 18 months after the November 2025 notification, but both rely on secondary summaries and the exact date should be confirmed in the gazette. Its list of organiser MVP items (event wizard, registrations table, custom fields, QR check-in, bibs, promo codes, broadcast, payouts ledger) matches the ranking here, and the main change is sequencing: guard, event workspace and write APIs come first because nothing else can be persisted without them. The landing-page report's guidance carries over directly: the "Live, In pilot, Coming soon" tags and "Sample data" labels it requires on marketing demos should be applied to every unfinished module in the dashboard, its mobile guidance (desktop-first, phones not blocked) is adopted for field mode, and its caution against copying IndiaRunning's unverified revenue-share and visitor claims applies here too. Townscript, used as a one-login precedent earlier, was reported shut down in the landing-page research with conflicting evidence, so it should not be used as a benchmark.

## What this research cannot establish

The audit is a code read; the app was never run, so mobile and accessibility remarks are structural, contrast ratios were not computed, and screen-reader behaviour of the mobile table cards is stated from known browser behaviour. The Prisma schema, `events.service.ts` in full, RLS policies on the 0025 and 0026 tables, and any `pg_cron` hold-release job were not checked, and `Feature status and gaps.md` predates the registrations migration. Competitor evidence has several limits. RunSignup's 2026 participant-list and reporting blogs returned HTTP 403, Challonge's pages returned 403, an Eventbrite recurring-events article returned 403 and another returned 404, ClearTax's GST page returned 410, Luma's host-dashboard help returned 404, and BookMyShow blocked fetching. Several RunSignup, Eventbrite and Race Roster claims (saved default columns, permissions definitions, app behaviour such as about six seconds per packet pickup) come from search-result summaries or app-store listings rather than fully read pages. No logged-in Indian organiser console (IndiaRunning, BookMyShow, Wowsly, Hudle) was observed, so Indian race-day tooling is inferred from marketing and runner-facing pages. LeagueApps, SportsEngine, Mindbody-admin and Hudle claims are second-hand.

The India-specific points are the weakest. GST rates and exemptions, the 0.5% TCS and 0.1% TDS figures, the DLT header rules (including vendor-reported 2025 amendments), WhatsApp India rates (vendors disagree), DPDP Rules 8, 10 and the Fourth Schedule, and breach and retention rules all come from law-firm, vendor or news summaries; the CBIC, TRAI, MeitY and Meta primary texts were not fetched. Whether a race entry fee is "admission to a sporting event", the agent-versus-principal question for s.52 and s.9(5), who deducts TDS, the organiser-as-fiduciary and platform-as-processor split, and the reconciliation of GST retention against DPDP deletion all need a CA or lawyer. Razorpay Route and Cashfree documentation does not say how pending KYC interacts with settlement holds, so confirm with provider support. A great deal of the roadmap is the researchers' own proposal and not a documented competitor feature: the phase-aware Home, the five-role permission set, saved-view names, duplicate detection, the sensitivity-flag export protection, the offline roster and scan-log design, field-mode targets (56 px, 7:1, 500 ms), the 170 KB budget, the proposed bracket and batch schema, and every impact and effort rating. Email deliverability (SPF, DKIM, DMARC and bulk-sender rules), timer file formats and DNS/DNF codes, triathlon leg tooling, swimming heats, federation weight rules and chargeback handling were not researched at all.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. The dashboard is not short of design ideas; it is short of a trust boundary. Everything an organiser needs depends on three missing things at once: an authorised organiser identity, write paths for event setup, and a payments provider that makes revenue and confirmed counts real. Until those exist, even a polished home screen would show zeros, and the demo's invented percentages are worse than zeros because they look like features. The efficient path is therefore to build the guard and the event workspace first, reuse the tables and RPCs that already exist (including `search_participants`, `event_daily_stats` and the publish trigger's list of missing items as the readiness checklist), and treat race-day check-in, exports and sensitive-data controls as part of the MVP, not as later polish, because they are where an organiser's reputation and a participant's privacy are actually at stake.

The owner's account decisions simplify the product as much as they constrain it. With no role switcher and no club, academy, coach or seller content, the organiser console can be one coherent workspace with one permission model built on tables that already exist, and the separate registration pages for other roles remove the pressure to make shared components serve everyone. The open questions that now matter most are not design ones: which payments provider and when, which timer file formats the first organisers use, what a CA says about TCS, TDS and ticket GST, and whether one email may hold several accounts. Settle those, and the ranked list above can be built in order without surprises.

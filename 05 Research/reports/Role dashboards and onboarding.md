# Build role-specific workspaces (originally: one login)

> **Decision update, 2026-11-18.** The product owner decided that each role has its own account: participant, organiser, seller, and
> coach or academy, each with its own sign-in and onboarding. This supersedes the one-login recommendation below. Where this report
> says "one login", "one identity" or "role switcher", read "one account per role". The payee entity, staged onboarding, compliance
> checklist and dashboard specs still apply, per account. See ADR-010 in `03 Decisions/`. One open point from that decision: whether
> one email can hold more than one account.

GoAthletix should give each person one login and let them activate separate workspaces for organiser, coach, academy and seller. The workspaces should be separate pages, onboarding flows and navigation. Money-earning KYC should attach to a separate payee entity, not to the login. IndiaRunning and BookMyShow both split organisers from consumers at the front door, but the evidence that they use separate identities behind that door is thin. IndiaRunning links "Create Event" to a separate organiser app, and its terms define Participants and Organizers as distinct account types. BookMyShow routes live-event organisers through a sales-assisted "list your show" enquiry. Neither platform's logged-in organiser screens could be seen. The stronger evidence for the identity model comes from Airbnb and Upwork (one person, several modes or profiles) and from Razorpay Route and Cashfree (payment recipients modelled apart from any login). Participants should get a mobile-first, light-touch experience. Organisers should get a desktop-first workspace that doubles as a race-day tool. Coaches and academies start in the no-payments "find a coach" phase that ADR-008 already specifies. Sellers wait for Phase 4, which ADR-007 and the product doc place after the first three roles. Tax, DPDP and child-safeguarding rules in this report come mostly from secondary sources and need sign-off from a CA or lawyer before they are built into compliance logic.

## Separate front doors are the norm, separate identities are not proven

The Indian benchmarks agree on the front door. IndiaRunning's header offers "Log In" and "Sign Up" for participants and a separate "Create Event" link to organiser.indiarunning.com, an app titled "India Running Organiser Dashboard" ([IndiaRunning](https://indiarunning.com)). The homepage has no link for sellers, coaches, clubs or volunteers. The T&Cs define Participants and Organizers (third parties who list, manage and promote events) as separate parties, and the privacy policy lists exactly two account types ([T&Cs](https://www.indiarunning.com/tnc); [Privacy Policy](https://www.indiarunning.com/privacy-policy)). Whether those two share a credential store is unknown, because the organiser app is client-rendered and returned only its title.

BookMyShow blocked automated fetching, so the evidence is secondhand. Its organiser entry is reported as a "Partner with us & get listed" page, and organisers do not self-sign-up: they submit a proposal and hear back within 24-48 working hours, according to a competitor's comparison page ([Ticket Fairy](https://www.ticketfairy.com/compare/ticketing/bookmyshow-alternatives)). Its public help centre has no partner category, and cinema staff use a separate, access-restricted "BookMyShow Partner" app ([help centre](https://bookmyshow.freshdesk.com/support/solutions); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1336023106)). A consumer can also resell tickets from inside the ordinary account (Your Order, then List on Marketplace), so resale is a consumer feature rather than a seller role ([BookMyShow help](https://bookmyshow.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/4000225692-how-do-i-list-my-tickets-)).

Across the other platforms studied, four patterns appear. Venue-operations tools (Playo, Hudle) ship a separate partner app and console with separate partner credentials ([Playo Partner Console](https://playo-partner.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/1070000137391-what-is-the-playo-partner-console-); [Hudle Partner](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id1569130632)). Community tools (Meetup, Strava) keep one account and attach the role to the group ([Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/blog/expand-your-meetup-group-through-collaborative-leadership/); [Strava](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402172-clubs-on-strava)). Marketplaces (Amazon, Flipkart, Decathlon) use a separate seller identity tied to a legal entity. Ticketing (Townscript) keeps organiser mode inside the same user account behind a KYC gate ([Townscript KYC](https://support.townscript.com/support/solutions/articles/1000156343-how-to-add-update-kyc-details-)). The Townscript point is implied by a "Billings" menu under "Organizing Events" and is not documented outright. None of the Indian sports platforms surfaced a first-class coach role: coaches appear as customers of venue software or as staff of a brand such as cult.fit.

The identity question is therefore a design choice, not something copyable from IndiaRunning or BookMyShow. The best-documented one-login precedents are Airbnb, where a single account toggles between traveling and hosting ([Airbnb Help](https://www.airbnb.co.in/help/article/3546)), and Upwork, where one account can hold a freelancer profile, an agency profile and several client profiles, each with its own contracts and finances ([Upwork](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211067558-Be-a-Client-and-a-Freelancer)). Both discourage duplicate accounts, and Airbnb offers no native way to merge host accounts or move reviews between them ([Guesty](https://www.guesty.com/blog/how-to-merge-airbnb-accounts/), a vendor blog). That is the cost of the separate-accounts option. A coach who also runs races would end up with two reputations and two consent histories. A separate-accounts model is justified only for hard isolation, such as junior participants under a guardian.

## Money roles need a payee entity that outlives any login

The strongest sourced argument for a hybrid model concerns where KYC lives. Razorpay Route, the provider ADR-007 names as the usual Indian choice, models a recipient as a "Linked Account" (the business) plus a "stakeholder" (the individual owner or signatory), with no reference to a login ([Razorpay Route guide](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/integration-guide.md)). The state machine is explicit. A linked account is created, a stakeholder is added, bank details are supplied, and the account either activates or returns `needs_clarification` with a list of requirements. Cashfree Easy Split uses a parallel path: `IN_KYC_REVIEW`, then `ACTIVE` or `ACTION_REQUIRED` ([Cashfree vendor KYC](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/payments/split/vendor/kyc.md)). GoAthletix's own `draft, submitted, under_review, active, action_required` states can map one-to-one onto these, so the UI never has to translate between platform and provider status.

The required documents depend on the payee type. For Razorpay Route an individual needs the signatory's PAN and a bank account. A proprietorship needs a bank account, with business PAN and GST optional. Companies, LLPs, partnerships, trusts, societies and educational institutes need business PAN, a bank account and GST ([Razorpay Route guide](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/route/integration-guide.md)). Because an academy is a society, trust or company with several staff and one bank account, the payee has to sit above any individual user. A coach who leaves an academy would otherwise take the academy's payout setup, or their own certifications and reviews, with them. This is also why the entity belongs in the model from day one, even though the organiser is the first role to need it.

Indian precedent shows two gates, not one. IndiaRunning's terms ask organisers for legal name, registered address, PAN, bank details and signatory ID, make KYC mandatory, and hold settlements until it is verified. They describe no formal pre-approval, and the platform may edit or delist listings afterwards ([T&Cs](https://www.indiarunning.com/tnc)). Townscript collects Aadhaar, bank details and GST status, verifies in 24-48 hours, and re-verifies on any KYC edit ([Townscript KYC](https://support.townscript.com/support/solutions/articles/1000156343-how-to-add-update-kyc-details-)). Its first clearing for a new organiser can take about a week, per a search snippet. The practical reading is that listing can be open and payout is the gate. Services that need trust before exposure, such as coaches working with children, are gated before listing instead. The same difference separates IndiaRunning's listing-first organisers from Urban Company's background-checked professionals.

Some figures cited in the notes need caution. The note on IndiaRunning's fee terms says the platform "may retain roughly 30% to 50%" of event revenue, but that came from an AI-summarised fetch the note itself says to re-read directly. This report does not rely on it.

## The new research mostly confirms the vault, with five places that extend or contradict it

The existing vault notes already make the biggest decisions, and the new research supports them. ADR-007 commits to platform ticketing with organiser and seller payouts and a provider with split settlements. The feature inventory says one login can hold organiser, seller and coach roles with "rights per entity, not global". ADR-008 phases coaches into a no-payments "find a coach" release first. The Airbnb and Upwork evidence, together with the Razorpay and Cashfree models, backs all three. SPORTIFI's authenticated console (Profile/KYC with GSTIN and bank, Settlements, Team) matches Townscript's KYC and the dashboard shape of RunSignup and Eventbrite. The registration data model is consistent as well: guest checkout with a nullable `user_id`, and organiser-only access to sensitive answers (`blood_group`, emergency contact and similar), already assume that a participant's data and a user's identity are separate objects.

The research extends the vault in three ways. First, the payee entity is new. The vault mentions `seller_kyc`, `seller_payout_accounts` and organiser Profile/KYC separately, but it does not propose one reusable payee object shared by organiser, seller, coach and academy. Razorpay's linked-account model argues for exactly that, with each organiser event or seller sale referencing a payee. Second, junior participants are only half-solved. The registration model captures a date of birth and a guardian field per ticket, but the DPDP Rules 2025 require verifiable parental consent for under-18s, and the notes recommend a dependent profile owned by a guardian rather than a separate login. The notes cite one source putting Rule 10's commencement at 13 May 2027, and another set of commentary at about 14 May, so the exact date needs confirming against the gazette ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1713500/regulatory-update-notification-of-the-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified-309928)). That is about six months from today. Third, the registration model attaches participants to an event and an order, not to a profile. A participant dashboard that shows "my registrations" therefore needs a way to claim guest orders by verified phone or email after a person signs up.

The research contradicts or complicates the vault in two places. The vault's club and academy note cites the POCSO Rules 2020 as requiring police verification and periodic background checks for staff of institutions such as sports academies, via a Business Standard snippet. The new notes found no Indian rule mandating background checks for private sports coaches on a platform. They found only NIS Patiala's certificate verification and a National Coach Accreditation Board that was still being structured in June 2026 ([NIS guidelines](https://nsnis.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/GUIDELINES.pdf); [NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/proper-structure-roadmap-being-prepared-national-coach-accreditation-681.htm)). These findings are not strictly in conflict, since one covers institutions and the other covers individual platform coaches. Both are secondary, so the correct treatment is to keep ADR-008's rule that under-18 listings stay hidden until counsel decides the evidence. The new notes also suggest tiered badges (identity, certificates, optional police verification) as platform policy, not law. The second complication is the coach console. The dashboard note proposes an "Earnings" item for coaches, but ADR-008 Phase 1 takes no money. The Phase 1 console should therefore end at Leads, and Earnings should appear only when Phase 2 payments arrive.

One further tension is about listing review. The vault's recommendation is to verify the coach once, review the first listing, and re-review on changes to price, schedule, venue, age group or safety. The new research does not contradict this and supports putting the strictest gate on listings that involve minors. It also offers a comparison point. IndiaRunning lets organisers list without pre-approval and keeps the right to delist, whereas ADR-007 requires review before sale for seller products. That asymmetry is defensible: counterfeit risk justifies review for products, while organisers get a lighter touch and a payout gate.

## Recommendation: one identity, role workspaces, staged gates

### Identity model

Use Supabase `auth.users` as the person and a global `profiles` row for name, contact, locale and consent records. Add one `role_profiles` row per activated role (participant, organiser, coach, seller), each with its own `draft, submitted, under_review, active, action_required` status. Add `organisations` (academies, clubs, organiser companies) with `memberships(role)` for owner, admin, staff and accountant, kept separate from any one person's history. Add `payee_entities` holding legal name, entity type, PAN, GSTIN, bank account and the provider's linked-account ID, referenced by organiser events, seller sales and coach or academy payouts. Minors are dependent profiles owned by a guardian's profile, with no login of their own. A person who is both organiser and participant is allowed. Organisers entering their own event are flagged but not blocked, and payouts always go to the payee tied to the event or sale, not to the role. Everyone starts as a participant.

For access control, the Supabase pattern is a role table, a Custom Access Token Hook that writes the role claim, and an `authorize()` function used in RLS policies ([Supabase RBAC](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/api/custom-claims-and-role-based-access-control-rbac)). The hook modifies only the access token, so clients must decode `session.access_token` to read it. Put coarse facts in the token (for example `roles` and `active_org_ids`), and check fast-changing ones, such as KYC status and membership removal, in the database from NestJS guards, because token claims go stale until refresh. The notes did not retrieve Supabase guidance on `app_metadata` versus `user_metadata` for authorisation, so confirm that before relying on either. Because the NestJS backend uses the service role and bypasses RLS (ADR-007), every guard must enforce role plus organisation membership itself.

### Entry points and routes

Keep one auth system and give each role its own landing page and signup intent. The header should carry "List your event" (organiser) and "Training" (coach and academy), matching the inert entries the vault already plans. Sellers stay out of the header until Phase 4 and get a waitlist form instead. Use path-based workspaces: `/dashboard/organiser`, `/dashboard/coach`, `/dashboard/academy/[orgId]`, `/dashboard/seller`, with participant pages at the root. The notes argue that subdomains like IndiaRunning's add cookie and CORS complexity without much benefit for a single login. Show a role and organisation switcher in the header, remember the last context, and keep participant notifications apart from business ones.

### Staged onboarding and first run

| Role | Stage 1: signup | Stage 2: activate | Stage 3: gate | First-run state and primary CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Participant | Phone or email OTP, name, optional age band | Profile completion deferred to first registration (emergency contact, blood group, T-shirt size) | None; guardian consent when adding a child | Empty state "Find your first event" with sport, city and date chips and a nearby carousel. **Find an event.** Secondary card: add a family member |
| Organiser | Same signup, arriving with organiser intent | Organisation or individual, contact, then draft event with autosave | Free events publish without KYC. Paid events publish once KYC is submitted. Payout waits for verified payee (PAN, bank, GST where required) | Setup checklist with progress bar, sample event, sport templates. **Create your first event.** |
| Coach | Same signup | Profile (photo, bio, sports, levels, location) and certification upload | Staff verify identity and certificates before first public listing; minors' listings wait for the safeguarding decision | Checklist to 80% profile, then the batch builder. **Complete your profile**, then **Create a training** |
| Academy or club | Same signup | Claim or create the centre listing (via the existing `owner_id` claim path), add batches, name coaches | Staff verification, safeguarding declaration for minors; payee only in Phase 2 | **Publish your listing**, then add the first batch |
| Seller | Waitlist only (Phase 4) | Business profile, GST, PAN, bank, pickup address | Product review before sale, per ADR-007 | **Add your first product** |

The notes recommend that every empty state carry a one-line value statement, one primary button, a preview and a "Skip, explore demo" link. These first-run states are design recommendations. No public data was found on activation or drop-off for any platform, so treat them as hypotheses to test.

### Navigation per workspace

The participant workspace should be mobile-first with bottom tabs: Home, Explore, My Registrations (tickets and QR), Results and Certificates, and Profile (medical and emergency details, family profiles, saved events). The organiser workspace should be a desktop-first sidebar: Home, Events (with a per-event workspace), Participants, Check-in and Race Day, Marketing, Finance, Reports, and Team and Settings. SPORTIFI's console, RunSignup and Eventbrite point to the same set, and the vault's feature inventory already lists most of the modules. Inside an event the notes suggest Overview, Tickets, Form, Participants, Bibs, Check-in, Promo, Messages, Results and Reports. The coach workspace should be Home, Profile and Credentials, Trainings, Schedule, Leads, and Reviews later. The academy workspace adds Coaches and Members and, in Phase 2, Fees and Attendance. Cap each at eight items. These lists are inferred from Teachable, Playo, Hudle, RunSignup and Amazon patterns and are not verified from any logged-in Indian competitor.

### MVP versus later

For organisers, the MVP is what gates a paid registration, a statutory need or safety. That means the event wizard, the registrations table with search, filters and export, custom fields, QR ticket and offline-tolerant scan check-in, bib assignment, promo codes, segment broadcast, a payouts ledger with downloadable statements, and a basic sales graph. The participant search, QR and sensitive-answer storage already exist in the registrations model, but its own open items list the organiser search route, scan endpoint and form builder as unbuilt, along with rate limiting. The organiser dashboard is therefore the largest build item. Later work includes team and relay registration, transfers and deferrals, a refund rules engine, results and timing-chip import, granular staff roles, waitlists and API access. For participants, the MVP is tickets with QR, a registrations list, profile with emergency and medical details, saved events, junior profiles with guardian consent, and certificates and results. Training logs, Strava sync and social features come later. For coaches and academies, the MVP follows ADR-008 Phase 1: profile, credentials, listings, leads inbox and staff review. Fees, attendance and online payments come in Phase 2 and 3. Sellers are entirely later.

## Evidence gaps that limit how far this can be trusted

The biggest gap is the one the task started from. IndiaRunning's organiser app rendered only its title, so its signup, KYC steps, event wizard and payout views were never observed, and the runner-side profile and dashboard widgets were not seen either. No help-centre or walkthrough was found. BookMyShow blocked fetching entirely, so every organiser-side claim comes from a competitor's marketing page, a Slashdot profile, or app-store listings, and whether its organisers and consumers share an account is not stated anywhere. The notes also found no BookMyShow phone-OTP signup source and no live-event organiser dashboard description. The nearest verified Indian organiser console is SPORTIFI's, which the vault documents from an authenticated walk-through, so the organiser navigation here leans on that plus RunSignup and Eventbrite help pages (several returned 403 and were read through search snippets).

Coach and academy benchmarks are the thinnest. No Indian platform studied documents a coach profile with batches, trial classes, enquiries or reviews. Playo and Hudle documentation covers venues only, cult.fit's trainer app is a staff tool, and the best structured model found is Playtomic's Academy module in Spain, used as a pattern reference only. Trial-class, enquiry and fee mechanics rest on the vault's UrbanPro and LyfSkills observations, some from snippets. Absence of public documentation is not proof that Playo or Hudle lack these features behind a login. Identity-model evidence is also uneven: Airbnb and Upwork are cited, but Uber, Etsy, Fiverr, Shopify and Eventbrite team structures were not retrieved, and no NN/g or Baymard research on role-switching or progressive onboarding was found, so the UX flows are opinion.

Tax and privacy points are secondary-source summaries, which is why the checklist above is labelled by confidence. The CBIC notification text for the 0.5% TCS and the ticket-GST changes was not retrieved, "recognised sporting event" is undefined in the sources, the Section 194-O individual threshold (commonly cited as Rs 5 lakh) was not verified, and PayU's split product and Razorpay's treatment of TDS and TCS were not researched. The DPDP commencement dates differ between sources, the children's-data exemptions were not checked against the gazette (G.S.R. 846(E)), and no source established a legal police-verification requirement for private sports coaches. Townscript's own page says its services were discontinued while another source found no shutdown, so it is an unreliable benchmark. A CA should confirm the GST, TCS and TDS positions, and counsel should confirm DPDP timing and safeguarding evidence, before any of this is encoded in checkout or payout logic.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the original question. IndiaRunning and BookMyShow show that an organiser needs a different front door from a consumer, but the evidence that matters for the data model comes from payment providers: the person who logs in and the legal payee are separate objects. The vault's one-login, rights-per-entity decision is right, and adding a payee entity and organisation memberships makes the organiser, academy and coach roles all fit one design. That structure also handles the awkward cases, such as a coach who also races, an academy with several staff, and a child with a guardian.

The practical implication is a sequencing choice. The first gate for every money role is payout, not listing, which lets organisers and coaches start building before verification finishes. The exception is anything involving minors, where the safeguarding gate comes first and its evidence is a legal decision that nobody has yet made. Build the organiser workspace and participant claim-your-orders flow first, since the registrations model already has the data and the missing pieces are organiser sign-in, search, check-in and payout. Treat the coach and academy workspaces as a lighter listing-and-leads product until Phase 2 payments exist, and keep sellers on a waitlist.
